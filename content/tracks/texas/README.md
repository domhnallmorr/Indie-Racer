# Texas Tri-Oval

Select **Texas Tri-Oval** in Race Weekend setup. Practice, qualifying and race
sessions load this package's surface, AI, timing, pit lane and camera positions.
This is a driving/AI test circuit with basic surroundings, not a finished scenery
recreation. It shares the game's current environment and vehicle systems.

## AI pace calibration

Texas uses a provisional assisted-player benchmark from the 26 September 2026
runs: approximately 22.06 seconds and 383–390 km/h at 17 gallons. The player was
still reaching the limiter in sixth, so revisit the straight-line target after
testing taller gearing. This is gameplay calibration, not historical lap data.

`tools/texas_pace.py` retains the imported LP speed shape but maps its range to
387–399 km/h before fuel and driver adjustments. The original samples remain in
`imported_speed_mps`; rebuilding through `tools/build_texas.py` reapplies the same
calibration. Geometry, racing grooves and banking are not changed by this step.

Greg Ray's roster value anchors the reference pace. Texas retains 45% of the
roster-relative lap-time differences, with driver scaling weighted over the
profile's own corner-to-straight speed range. This avoids treating every sample
on a nearly flat-out oval as a straight. Other tracks retain legacy weighting
and their full roster spread. Texas's passing speed multiplier is neutral (1.0).

The first 18-car, seed-42 check completed 124 laps in 240 simulated seconds,
starting every car at 20 gallons. Representative unrestricted laps ending near
16–17.5 gallons were 21.98 s / 380–392 km/h for Greg Ray, 22.07 s / 379–390 km/h
for Jeff Ward, 22.27 s / 375–387 km/h for Robbie McGehee, and 22.59 s /
369–384 km/h for Hattori. Pit-exit/merge and the Mile Oval roster regression
checks passed. The profile calibration is idempotent and retains line alignment.

Known limitation: the full-field check also reproduced two hard static-surface
impacts in Turn 3 (Hornish around 153 s, Sharpe around 218 s), despite small
tracking error immediately before impact. Both recorded no car contact. These
laps are excluded from clean-pace comparisons; this calibration does not fix
the surface-contact issue or establish incident-free full-field racing.

## Viewing in the Godot editor

Open `content/tracks/texas/scenes/track.tscn` from the FileSystem panel and
switch to the **3D** workspace. The track generates its surfaces, walls, ground
and pit numbers in the editor, so you can inspect it without starting a session.
Select the **Texas** root and press **F** to focus, then use the mouse wheel to
zoom, middle-drag to orbit, or hold the right mouse button and use **WASD** to
fly around. If the track is clipped at a distance, increase **View Settings >
View Far** in the 3D viewport. Reopen the scene after rebuilding its geometry data.

The generated track is a preview; add scenery as new children of the Texas root.
Its road geometry still comes from `geometry.json`, and collisions are generated
when the game runs.

## Walls and catch fence

The outer wall also carries a full-circuit catch fence (`CatchFence` in the scene).
`catchfence.gd` reuses Mile Oval's steel posts, rails and diamond-wire shader,
with 4 m high fencing and an inward-leaning upper section. Its base interpolates
the actual Texas wall top, following both bank profiles and closing at the lap
seam without a duplicate post. Like the Mile Oval fence, it is visual scenery;
the concrete wall supplies collision. It generates in the editor and at runtime.

The `InnerWall` scene child builds a continuous 1.15 m high, 0.5 m thick
concrete boundary along the infield edge of the apron, behind the pit stalls.
It shares Mile Oval's weathered concrete wall shader, using distance-based UVs
for joints along the Texas layout. Runtime collision follows the visible wall;
the grass between the racing surface and pit road remains open. The wall is
generated by `inner_wall.gd` and is also visible in the editor.

Along the straight pit stalls the wall's track-facing side sits at Z=193 m,
4 m behind the stall centres and 1 m behind their painted rear edges. The
`PitStations` child reuses Mile Oval's canopies, desks and equipment for all
27 bays, positioned behind the wall with 0.2 m roof clearance. Runtime roster
colours use the existing station assignment hook; the pace-car station is gold.

## Frontstretch grandstand

`grandstand.gd` builds the `FrontstretchGrandstand` scene child in both the editor
and the game. This photo-inspired approximation spans about 714 m from the turn-4
exit to the turn-1 entry, with 48 stepped seating rows, 30 seating bays, concrete
aisles, and a continuous three-storey glass-fronted suite building. White fascia,
blue rooftop accents and speedway lettering follow the supplied reference.
It reuses the existing crowd atlas and combines geometry into seven material
meshes. The stand is visual scenery, without additional collision bodies.

Placement comes from the generated outer road edge, smoothed at section joins
to keep the deep seating bowl continuous around the dogleg.
Seating and suites follow the smoothed outer-road elevation, maintaining their
height above the banked ends; foundation feet remain at ground level.
Solid front cladding and stepped end walls close the seating underside, with
foundation feet sunk slightly below the ground plane to prevent visible gaps.
Adjust `START`, `END`, `BAYS` and `ROWS` in the script for future scenery work. Dimensions are
an artistic approximation, not a surveyed reconstruction.

## Backstretch grandstand

`BackstretchGrandstand` is a separate, roofless 360 m bleacher stand outside
the backstraight catch fence. Its 20 rows of bare aluminium benches have stair
aisles, handrails, a rear walkway and exposed grounded steel supports. It uses
three combined material meshes, without crowd textures or spectators. The
period-reference approximation is generated by `backstretch_grandstand.gd` in
both the editor and game; it is visual scenery with no additional collisions.

## Infield surfaces

The `InfieldSurfaces` child provides ground scenery from the supplied map:
patchy olive turf, pale paddock hardstanding, tan garage aprons, service roads
and transporter parking paint. The backstraight-side infield road course uses
darker, maintained asphalt for the early-2000s interpretation, rather than the
deteriorated appearance in the modern imagery. Its route is an artistic visual
approximation, not a surveyed or playable circuit layout with its own AI.
`infield.gd` builds seven surface meshes and `infield_surface.gdshader` provides
metre-scaled procedural grain, colour variation and subtle concrete joints.
The pit-lane grass uses the maintained-turf variant. The underlying ground
collision is retained; these decorative surfaces add no physics bodies.

## Asphalt and lane paint

`asphalt.gdshader` supplies metre-scaled aggregate, broad weathering, longitudinal
paving seams and a feathered rubber groove following `ai/race_line.json`.
Rubber is heavier through the turns and lighter along the straights. Fine grain
fades with screen footprint to reduce distant shimmer. The apron and pit paving
share the base asphalt without racing rubber or white lane separators.

Two dashed white separators sit at one-third and two-thirds of the racing
surface width, through the front tri-oval and backstraight only. Turn limits
come from the banking metadata; the front pattern continues across the lap
seam. The initial artistic dimensions are 150 mm paint width, 3 m dashes and
9 m gaps. These are material markings, so they follow the banked mesh without
floating overlays or collision changes. Road UVs encode lap distance and
lateral position; UV2 encodes the authored groove and local road width.

## Race grid grounding

Grid slots sample the generated racing-surface triangles at the upright chassis
footprint, adding 25 mm clearance above the highest sampled corner. This keeps
both starting lanes above the five-degree bank instead of using the legacy flat
grid-origin height. Normal tyre grounding settles the visual onto the road.
The shared grid transform also applies this placement on formation resets.
`tools/validate_texas_grid_grounding.gd` checks all 26 slots against collision,
player/AI tyre support after settling, and the unchanged Mile Oval grid height.

## Source and geometry

Built from the user-supplied `ntexas(1).zip`. Its readme credits **Tom Smith** with
converting the NASCAR 3 Texas track to ICR2. `source.json` records the archive hash,
original reference length and transformations. No archive installation programs
are run, and no source scenery, textures or executable code are imported.

The builder decodes `TEXAS.TRK` inside `TEXAS.DAT` and maps `RACE.LP`, `PASS1.LP`,
`PASS2.LP` and `PIT.LP` longitudinal/lateral coordinates into the TRK plan.
Each original LP contains 728 records. The decoder's format reference is
[SK Chow's ICR2Tools](https://github.com/skchow03/icr2tools).

- The original 2419.337 m reference is uniformly scaled to **2414.016 m / 1.5 miles**.
- Straights have **5 degrees** banking, rising outward from the level apron.
- Turns 1/2 have **20 degrees** banking; turns 3/4 have **24 degrees**.
- Banking rises/falls over **360 m**, extending only **40 m onto each adjoining
  straight**. Most of the transition lies inside the turn. Quintic easing gives
  continuous first and second derivatives at the ramp ends. Approximately
  **325 m of the backstraight holds a constant 5-degree bank**, and the two maximum-bank plateaus
  total about **72 m**, concentrated near the middle of each turn pair.
- The road is 19 m wide, with an inside paved apron. Both rendering and collision
  use the same roughly 2 m longitudinal samples and transverse surface grid.
- The race groove retains imported LP offsets, with a 12 m smoothing window.
  Passing grooves retain the shared LP shape but are separated by 7.6 m within
  the car-centre corridor. This leaves a usable alternative to RACE with the
  planner's 3.2 m lateral reserve; 5.2 m between PASS lanes alone was insufficient.
  The original passing lines are only about 3 m apart.
- The imported PIT.LP entry and exit blend into an authored straight service lane
  at Z=205 m, roughly 38 m inside the track's central inner edge (about twice the
  previous gap). All 26 race stalls and the pace-car stall share Z=197 m and a
  -90 degree heading, spaced 9 m apart from X=-117 to +117 m. The pit markings,
  AI path, limiter zone, paved apron and infield wall move with this layout.
- Pit road has open grass separation from the track, without a separator wall.
  The grass tapers out before the paved pit entry/exit connections; the flat
  apron beneath it provides ground collision.

The archive predates the requested asymmetric banking. This is therefore its
historic plan combined with the requested banking, not a surveyed modern Texas.

## AI pace

Imported RACE.LP speeds are retained as an initial reference, not calibrated to
the player's physics or the changed banking. They can exceed 400 km/h. Existing
roster lap targets are converted into relative pace differences around this
track's reference time; Mile Oval times are not used as absolute Texas targets.
The ICR2 AI uses kinematic reference following, so successful circulation does
not establish that the player's tyre model can reproduce those speeds.

Fuel use per metre is preserved, while lap-based fuel strategy uses the longer
circuit. Visual scenery and player/AI pace calibration remain future work.

The full builder uses the shared profile in `texas_banking.py`. Running that
script directly updates the current package without needing the source archive:
road, outer wall, edge lines, racing paths, timing-gate heights and banking

source geometry automatically. The archive-free update preserves the straight
pit layout. `validate_texas_banking.gd` checks both maximum banks, the 5-degree
front and back straights, reference-path collision heights and catch-fence attachment.

The field probe checks both bank angles, profile/corridor loading, timed laps,
ground contact, road bounds and pit limiter compliance. The session probe checks
formation-to-green and an AI pit service followed by rejoining the race. Reports
and screenshots are written under `builds/`.

### Pit exit

`layout_texas_pits.py` keeps the departure path 8 m inside the asphalt's inner
edge through turns 1/2 and along the left apron to circuit station 1200 m.
The driver's tangent-matched merge then joins the racing line over about 120 m,
crossing onto the track near the middle of the backstraight. The painted guide
follows this path. The pit-out speed profile ramps towards racing speed over
the final 600 m, while preserving the stall departure and pit speed limits.

Path-based merge checks predict traffic throughout the lateral crossing and
use circuit distance to include cars still rounding turn two. Blocked cars
wait on the apron and reconsider clearance each tick; a committed merge keeps
accelerating. `validate_texas_pit_exit.gd` checks the delayed crossing, speed,
yield/release from rest, a faster car catching the crossing before the final
join, and recovery after a physical rear impact. The seed-42 fixture crossed
at approximately 337 km/h and accelerated again after contact.

With this exit, the 200-second full-field stress probe completed 5–6 timed laps
per car, with zero grounding or limiter failures. Its road-boundary assertion
still failed: Buhl left the road at turn one around 184 seconds (233 off-road
ticks) and recovered before the run ended. This broader traffic failure remains
open; the focused pit-exit, pit-service and existing merge/following probes pass.

### Initial results (original 120 m banking transitions)

Measured turn collision normals give 20.002° and 23.999°. The 18-car 2001 IRL
field circulated for five simulated minutes, completing 9–11 timed laps each,
with no lost ground contact or pit-limiter violations. Formation-to-green,
pit service and rejoining also passed, as did the existing Mile Oval timing and
race-start regression checks.

The stricter road-boundary stress check **does not yet pass**: in an accelerated
pit-release run, Sharpe recovered backwards after traffic contact near the
turn-1 entry and briefly crossed onto the inside apron. The field continued
circulating. Peak speeds were about 410 km/h. This exposes an AI traffic/pace
tuning issue rather than establishing player-physics feasibility. Keep the
boundary check enabled when tuning; its JSON report records the excursions.



### Pit tuning from 2 October practice telemetry

This supersedes the pit tuning described above. Texas AI now commits about
950 m before the pit-path origin, blends inward over 250 m onto the flat apron,
and targets 235 km/h through turns 3/4. Initial braking follows distance travelled
from entry speed. A separate braking envelope targets the actual 80 km/h limiter
zone, rather than the beginning of the pit path. Stall braking previews the
8 m/s turn instead of stepping down abruptly at 40 m.

Exit cruise is 185 km/h, accelerating towards racing speed over the final 700 m.
The AI begins its merge 120 m before the painted guide ends and blends to the
racing line over about 240 m. The painted guide is unchanged. The seed-42 test
crosses at circuit station 1141 m at approximately 333 km/h. The merge predictor
protects the full racing corridor against traffic arriving from turn two.
Other tracks retain their existing pit settings.

These are initial settings based on the phases of the player's recorded driving,
not a replay of the player's inputs. Validate practice entry, apron cruising,
stall return and repeat departure with validate_practice_pits.gd -- --texas.
validate_texas_pit_exit.gd covers blocked/clear merges, fast approaching traffic
and contact recovery. validate_texas_session.gd covers refuelling and rejoining.
