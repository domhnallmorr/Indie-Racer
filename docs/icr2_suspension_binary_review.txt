Confirmed observations

The initialized data contains RF Shock, RR Shock, LR Shock and LF Shock.
The menu at 0x68250 selects those labels from the pointer table at data
0x105A4. Its increment/decrement callbacks at 0x68124 and 0x68148 call
getter 0x13D40 and setter 0x13C94, with steps of five.

Setter 0x13C94 clamps the requested setting to 0..100, maps wheel index
through 0x1353C, stores the setting in data 0x25BF4, and computes
clamp(2047 + trunc(setting*2048/100), 2047, 4095). It writes that coefficient
to two words in the indexed 8-byte records beginning at data 0x8760,
then calls 0x18BC4. These numbers are internal coefficients, not recovered
spring rates in N/m or damping rates in N*s/m.

Routine 0x18BC4 adds corner coefficients and two further setup coefficients
at 0x8780/0x8782. It stores totals and fixed-point ratios at 0x878A..0x8792.
The adjacent setter 0x13D50 clamps its own setting to 1..8 and writes
2047 + 768*setting to 0x8780 + 2*index; its menu identity is unverified here.

The running routine at 0x15E94 uses those totals and ratios. It updates
persistent signed 16-bit state at 0x8714 and 0x8716 using target-minus-state
terms and fixed-point coefficients. Paired components at 0x8718..0x871E
are added/subtracted from baseline corner quantities, producing four loads
at 0x870C..0x8712. Each is clipped to zero if negative. Those loads feed
the tyre response paths documented in the earlier tyre investigation.

Later arithmetic at 0x161BE..0x163F3 divides corner baseline quantities by
the individual shock coefficients and combines the results with transfer
terms. Several outputs are bounded to 0..574. This is consistent with
corner deflection/pose calculation, but their physical meaning and consumers
have not been fully traced.

Interpretation and limits

Strong evidence: per-corner shock setup affects a compact stateful transfer
and wheel-load model. It is more than a purely visual menu setting.
Plausible interpretation: an economical suspension compliance/settling
approximation with derived corner pose. The name 'Shock' alone does not
prove a physically separate spring and velocity-dependent damper model.
Not established: independent wheel vertical velocities, unsprung masses,
spring/damper force equations, bump stops, road-height excitation, camber
kinematics, SI conversions, or absence of those features elsewhere.

Project recommendation

The player already evaluates four tyre loads and individual tyre forces,
making suspension a useful next development. Existing longitudinal and
lateral transfer filters are approximations; road sampling at four corners
currently aligns the visual mesh only. CharacterBody floor snap and the
surface-step helper still control physical support.

Start with an isolated dynamic roll/pitch prototype that reproduces the
current steady-state load distribution. Then add heave, physical corner road
samples, effective wheel springs/dampers, anti-roll coupling and travel limits.
The suspension-derived loads must replace the existing transfer calculation,
not be added to it. Define which system owns vertical support so floor snap
does not suppress suspension motion or maintain contact over crests.

Keep the current tyre curve and differential settings fixed during comparison.
Validate parked equilibrium, braking/turn-in settling, a single-wheel bump,
banking, contact loss, and timestep convergence. The tyre curve already has
unresolved historical slide-regression bounds, so preserve the current baseline
before making suspension the default. Defer unsprung-mass and camber complexity
until the simpler model is stable and useful in driving comparisons.
