Indianapolis roll/pitch test -- 10 October 2026

Historical stage-1/2 notes. Current stage-3 driving instructions and travel
validation are in docs/indy_suspension_travel.txt.

Start Indianapolis in Private Testing. The human player's dynamic roll/pitch
prototype is initially enabled. AI and other tracks retain existing physics.
While parked at the pit stall, open Edit Car Setup -> Roll Balance. Uncheck
Dynamic roll and pitch and apply to compare with the previous transfer filters.
This preference saves per track in user://mechanical_setups.cfg; enabling or
disabling resets vehicle dynamics while parked. Existing roll balance remains
independently adjustable. F5 shows the body angles; telemetry adds active state,
angles, rates and reaction moments. The sidecar records the exact modal rates.

Prototype parameters and hypotheses

Configuration: content/vehicles/open_wheel/physics/indy_suspension.cfg
Base authored roll stiffness 180000 N*m/rad; pitch stiffness 180000 N*m/rad.
Effective roll inertia 1000 kg*m^2; pitch inertia 1000 kg*m^2, scaled by fuel mass.
The roll inertia is an effective reduced-mode parameter, not measured chassis data.
Damping ratio 0.8 for both modes. These are provisional, not ICR2 SI values.
The recovered ICR2 mapping is coefficient=2047+floor(setting*2048/100), bounded
to 2047..4095. We use the mean of the four coefficients divided by the
coefficient at setting 50 (3071) to scale both stiffnesses. This yields a
factor approximately 1.158. It is an explicitly unverified stiffness hypothesis.
The four settings are preserved, but this two-mode implementation aggregates
their effects; it does not recreate left/right shock asymmetry or cross-weight.
The existing front roll-balance setting determines the axle moment split.

Equations: I*q_ddot = applied_contact_moment - K*q - C*q_dot;
C=2*0.8*sqrt(K*I). Spring-plus-damper reaction determines transfer, replacing
the old filters rather than adding a second transfer. Longitudinal contact
force gives pitch excitation; lateral contact force gives roll excitation.
Steady-state reactions recover the old contact-force transfer equilibrium.
No aero pitch trim, heave, wheel travel, camber changes, bump stops, rollover,
unsprung mass, or independent wheel-height excitation is included. Airborne
states clear because these modes are constrained to a supported road plane.
Existing floor snap and surface-step support remain for this first stage.

Visuals

Chassis meshes rotate about a provisional CG-height pivot. Wheels stay in the
road-aligned visual frame; cockpit follows the chassis. This prevents artificial
wheel sinking or lifting from rotating the entire car mesh. Suspension links
are not articulated. Visual CG/axle geometry remains approximate.

Test procedure

Compare clean-air Indy runs at the same fuel, tyre condition, wings, gearing,
roll balance, assists and inputs. Focus on entry braking, steering reversals,
turn-in settling and exit recovery. Record with F11, or use automatic session
telemetry. Road bumps and independent kerb response are outside this stage.
Neither lap-time improvement nor ICR2 handling equivalence has been established.

First driven evaluation and roll-compliance correction

Latest reviewed recording: practice_2026-10-10T12-54-22_9750.csv, Indianapolis,
362.48 seconds / 21749 rows; dynamic roll/pitch active throughout.
Above 250 km/h while grounded, absolute body roll had a 95th percentile of
9.525 degrees and a maximum of 9.962 degrees. Absolute pitch peaked at
0.649 degrees. The driver reported excessive visual roll.

Profile v2 scales roll stiffness AND effective roll inertia by four relative
to v1. Damping coefficient automatically scales by four as well. For the same
contact-moment history this quarters angle and angular rate while preserving
spring-plus-damper reaction, modal frequency, damping ratio and tyre transfer.
This avoids simultaneously retuning the handling from the first driven laps.
It projects the recorded high-speed peak from 9.962 to 2.491 degrees, relative
to the road plane; this is a same-input projection, not a fresh driven result.
Pitch settings and ICR2 reference shock values stay as before.

The modal test checks this invariance over turn-in, reversal and release at
fuel-adjusted mass. It passes, as do the coupled 60/120 Hz and Indy scene/UI
checks. A further driven session should assess the reduced chassis/cockpit lean.

Second driven evaluation

Reviewed practice_2026-10-10T13-07-10_10775.csv: 253.38 seconds / 15203 rows.
Sidecar confirms profile v2, effective roll K=208485.835 N*m/rad, I=1000 kg*m^2,
and active dynamic body modes. The driver reports improved visual roll.
High-speed grounded samples: roll p95=2.389 degrees and maximum=2.492 degrees,
versus 9.525/9.962 previously. This agrees with the quarter-angle correction.
After excluding pit/setup, off-tarmac and +/-2s around material wall impulses
and resets, turning samples (speed >250 km/h, |yaw| >5 deg/s) have median roll
2.243 degrees, p95=2.417, maximum=2.492. Pitch p95=0.402 degrees in those turns.

Comparable turning sideslip p95 is 3.157 degrees versus 3.195 before; maximum
3.526 versus 3.864. Roll reaction p95 is 8850.8 versus 8780.7 N*m; maximum
9254.6 versus 9314.2. No unloaded wheel, rear post-peak sample, rear slip above
12%, or sideslip excursion above 8 degrees appears in the filtered new samples.
Minimum filtered high-speed FL load is 822.9 N; all four loads stay positive.
Front tyres are post-peak for 10.37 of 95.45 filtered turning seconds (10.9%);
the earlier run gives 9.70 of 93.47 seconds (10.4%). This suggests a modest
front-limited balance remains, rather than a new rear stability problem.

Four full laps confirmed through all six gates: 40.014, 39.813, 39.677, 39.511s.
Only the 39.677s lap has no recorded wall impulse above 0.1 m/s; the other
three each contain one. All four remain on tarmac without resets.
Top speed is 381.644 km/h versus 372.544 before. The earlier timed laps use
sixth gear 1.19; the new timed laps use 1.16. Wings remain 4.7/4.05 degrees,
front roll share 50%, diff 30% drive/10% coast/20 N*m, stability and TC off,
ABS on, and no tow. Fuel burns in both runs; an end-of-session reset restored
35 gallons in the earlier recording, so start/end fuel alone is misleading.
The gearing difference prevents attributing faster speed/laps to suspension.

Evidence supports retaining v2 roll compliance for now. No additional physics
or setup tuning was made during this analysis. Machine-readable comparison:
