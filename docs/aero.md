# Player aero setup

While parked in your practice or qualifying pit box, use cockpit view (5),
press Enter or click the monitor, then choose **Edit Car Setup > Wings**. Choose a speedway or road
body package and independent front/rear wings from 3–18 degrees in 0.05-degree
steps. Remain parked in the box to apply changes. Applying also saves the setup for
the current track to `user://aero_setups.cfg`, shared across practice,
qualifying and race sessions. Unsaved edits are discarded when reopening the page.

The preview compares setups at 300 km/h in still air. **8 / Diagnostics** shows
current forces and aero balance. **F11** records telemetry; appended CSV columns
capture the active setup and forces every tick, including setup changes mid-run.

## Reconstruction and calibration

Source: `ICR2_Player_Car_Aerodynamic_Model_Summary.pdf` supplied by the user.
This implementation uses its reconstructed relationships, not independently
verified executable results or a validated physical reproduction of ICR2.

Internal body drag is 28,000 (speedway) or 33,000 (road). Body downforce is
2.60 or 2.55 times body drag, respectively, with one-third at the front axle.
For angle A in hundredths of a degree:

```
front wing drag = 50 + floor(13100 * (A - 300) / 1500)
rear wing drag  = 100 + floor(26200 * (A - 300) / 1500)
wing efficiency = 4.5 / (0.5 + (angle_degrees - 3) / 15)
wing downforce = wing drag * wing efficiency
```

Wing efficiency uses the report's continuous approximation. Each wing's
downforce goes to its axle. Body and wing coefficients are summed, then
multiplied by `coefficient_area_scale` to produce effective areas in m².
Physics applies `0.5 * air_density * relative_airspeed²` once to these areas.
The resulting downforce feeds tyre loads and steering assistance; drag opposes
relative air velocity. Rolling resistance still opposes ground velocity.

The default scale, 0.8 / 46489, matches the previous 0.8 m² drag area at
speedway 10°/10°. It is a provisional calibration, not an ICR2 unit conversion.
Defaults are speedway, front 14°, rear 8° on both current ovals. These are
starting values for tuning, not historical setups or lap-time targets. They
do not preserve the previous 3.6 m² downforce or 43% front balance exactly.

Edit `content/vehicles/open_wheel/physics/aero.cfg` to change the common scale,
air density or defaults. Saved per-track settings override only package and
wing angles, so future calibration changes also affect existing saved setups.
`aero_model.gd` contains the coefficient relationships. The config loader
derives `drag_area_m2`, `downforce_area_m2` and `front_downforce_fraction` for
existing consumers; these are no longer independent authored aero settings.

## Limits and next calibration pass

### Banking support correction

Tyre normal loads include the normal acceleration generated by turning on a
banked surface, using actual yaw rate and the contact road basis. In a steady,
level banked turn this adds `mass * speed² / radius * sin(bank)` to the
gravity-supported load. Turning against the bank can reduce that load. The
contribution is zero on flat ground and while airborne. It is distributed
between axles using the chassis weight distribution; aerodynamic load retains
its own front/rear distribution.

Steering assistance includes lateral gravity from the bank in its steering
and yaw limits, with separate limits for each direction. `banking_load_n` in
telemetry and Diagnostics reports the signed extra normal load. This corrects
an omitted source of grip without changing the aero coefficient scale or tyre
friction. This remains a road-plane approximation: vertical road curvature and
full suspension dynamics are not represented by this correction.

- Absolute drag/downforce still need calibration. Use straight-line acceleration,
  coast-down, corner speed and handling balance alongside lap times. A 26.8-second
  Texas lap alone cannot separate gearing, tyre grip and aero effects.
- Player `wind_world_mps` is world-space air velocity, zero by default. It is
  projected onto the road plane. This does not model wing yaw sensitivity,
  aerodynamic side force/moments, ride height, stall or drafting.
- The existing road-plane model applies downforce to tyre loads only while
  grounded. Airborne vertical aerodynamic forces are not simulated.
- AI trajectory speed profiles and gearing have not been retuned here.
- Runtime testing and driving calibration have not been performed for this change.
