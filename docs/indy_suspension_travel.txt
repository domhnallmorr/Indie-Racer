Apron and road-frame corrections (profile v5)

Review of practice_2026-10-10T14-47-07_8063.csv confirms increased rear slip
in some turns, alongside sharp road-frame events at the apron. This is not
enough to attribute the whole handling change to stiffness: driving inputs,
line, speed and transient road geometry differ from the accepted v2 run.
Steady four-corner load comparisons now explicitly cover 9-degree banking,
95 m/s at 0.2 rad/s yaw, aero and braking, at front roll shares .4/.5/.6.
They match the accepted transfer split within 0.1 N. No roll-balance, tyre,
differential, aero or gearing tune was applied to conceal the transient issues.

The road frame is fitted to the four sampled heights instead of averaging
discontinuous triangle normals. Before comparing successive frames, the old
normal is carried through the parent's yaw, so yaw on constant banking is not
a new pitch input. A frame rotation changes both measured gap and relative
body angle: their angular-rate terms cancel in physical compression rate.
Only actual road-point velocity and body heave/angular velocity enter damping.
This removes an extra damper input during road-frame transitions. Wheel meshes
follow geometrically reachable road even when delivered force momentarily
clamps to zero; zero force no longer sends both front wheels to full droop.

validate_suspension_apron.gd crosses a continuous-height, sharp-normal seam
between flat apron and a nine-degree bank, diagonal to the car. At 5 mm of
sideways travel per frame, maximum wheel-height step is 0.805 mm and frame-angle
step is 0.030 degrees. Rotating the car and road together produces less than
0.00005 rad/s spurious road-rate input. The model fixture separately checks
that coordinate relabelling cannot create spring/damper loads.

Clean high-speed samples in the driven v4 run (>280 km/h, rear slip <8 degrees,
all four loaded contacts) show weighted compression of 24 mm median / 40 mm
95th percentile. Some larger transients remain in that recording. Heave
stiffness stays at 400000 N/m while the transition corrections are evaluated;

Implementation

travel_suspension.gd extends the previous body modes. The accepted roll and
pitch stiffnesses, effective inertias and damping ratio remain the baseline.
Four corner springs/dampers plus positive heave and roll/pitch coupling springs
form a sprung-body model. Corner spring shares follow the static front weight
fraction; coupling rates complete the target heave/roll/pitch stiffnesses and
the existing selectable front roll share. This is an authored modal suspension
assembly, not a recovered ICR2 linkage or certified set of physical wheel rates.
No individual unsprung wheel masses are integrated.

For corner i, x_i is forward axle lever, y_i is leftward half track width,
g_i is sampled normal gap from body origin to the road, and z is normal body
displacement since the start of this tick:

  compression_i = ride_height - g_i + road_velocity_i*t - z
                  - x_i*pitch - y_i*roll
  compression_rate_i = road_velocity_i - heave_velocity
                       - x_i*pitch_rate - y_i*roll_rate

Road motion is sampled once per physics tick and extrapolated inside the
existing <=0.5 ms model substeps. The road rays use the authored visual tyre
contact positions; moments retain the existing physics wheelbase/track widths.
Those two geometries remain approximate rather than perfectly matched.
Changes in road-plane orientation transport the body angles instead of
instantly tilting the chassis. Road-point motion supplies the damper input;
coordinate-frame rotation alone must not excite damping.

Each delivered Fz_i is the positive spring/damper-plus-bump-stop reaction,
only when a road sample exists and compression is within droop reach. Loads
never pull on the road, and an unsupported wheel has zero tyre force. These
loads replace the previous transfer split. The body equations are:

  m*z_ddot = sum(Fz_i) - m*normal_support_accel - front_aero - rear_aero
  I_roll*roll_ddot = lateral_contact_force*cg_height + sum(y_i*Fz_i)
  I_pitch*pitch_ddot = longitudinal_contact_force*cg_height + sum(x_i*Fz_i)
                       - a*front_aero + b*rear_aero

Normal support uses the sampled world road-normal acceleration. All three
states integrate semi-implicitly. Freefall includes normal and tangent gravity,
so airborne motion on a bank still follows world-down. The old transfer filters
are not added to these forces. Static preload sets the reference normal-weight
distribution, while aero and banking load compress the suspension. Front/rear
aero imbalance now creates physical pitch trim; unlike stage 2, pitch is not
restricted to contact-force acceleration alone.

Authored stage-3 parameters

Loaded reference support height: 0.16 m. Total heave stiffness: 1200000 N/m.
Travel pitch stiffness scale: 6; roll stiffness/inertia scale: 2.
Damping ratio: 0.8. Compression limit before bump stop: +0.06 m.
Droop reach relative to reference: -0.05 m. Bump-stop stiffness: 3000000 N/m
per corner, beyond the compression limit. The bump stop is progressive force,
not a hard compression clamp. Parameters are in indy_suspension.cfg.
The ICR2 shock values remain LF 85, RF 70, LR 75, RR 65; their mean recovered
coefficient scales the authored roll/pitch rates. They are not SI wheel rates.

Game support and visuals

With travel active, actual chassis normal motion comes from suspension, and
floor snap plus the surface-step helper are bypassed. On a freshly placed car
near a road, initial support height is established once; a car placed beyond
droop falls normally. The collision box follows the sampled road orientation
for wall/car contacts and emergency chassis-ground contact. Its base remains
at the body origin, above the tyre plane, so banking cannot lift the chassis
by its uphill edge. It does not rotate with dynamic body roll/pitch. Disabling
travel restores the original collision transform.
Chassis meshes and cockpit follow the dynamic modes; each wheel independently
follows reachable road height or its droop position. Tyre spin remains separate.
The visual suspension arms are not articulated. Camber, per-wheel surface grip,
tyre vertical compliance, unsprung mass, and rollover are still unmodelled.
